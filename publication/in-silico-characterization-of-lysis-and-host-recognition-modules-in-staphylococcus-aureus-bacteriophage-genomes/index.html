In silico characterization of lysis and host-recognition modules in Staphylococcus aureus bacteriophage genomes

Ivan Armadi Hasugian+, Na'ilah Insani Alifiyah

Department of Biology, Faculty of Science and Technology, Universitas Terbuka, Tangerang Selatan, Indonesia

Unduh PDF 1,1 MB Halaman artikel DOI ORCID

Abstrak

Background
Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) is a leading human pathogen, and declining antibiotic efficacy has renewed interest in lytic bacteriophages, whose efficacy phenotypic assays predict poorly.
Objectives
To characterise the lysis and host-recognition modules of lytic S. aureus bacteriophages, and to determine how far the detectability of each module depends on the annotation source rather than on the genomes.
Methods
Twenty-two S. aureus phage genomes from NCBI GenBank were processed through an identical detection funnel under three annotation sources: the deposited record, Pharokka and Phold. Modules were validated by InterPro, disrupted endolysins resolved by tBLASTn, and each domain aligned separately to measure identity.
Findings
The LysK-type endolysin (CHAP-Amidase-SH3b) was conserved and detected identically under all three sources. Tail fibre/RBP detection was strongly source-dependent: 14 of 22 genomes from the deposited annotation, 20 of 22 after re-annotation, and 22 of 22 combined. Resolved by domain, conservation was scale-dependent: the amidase domain was invariant within species while SH3b varied by 5.7%.
Main conclusions
Lysis-module conservation is independent of the annotation source; the apparent variability of host recognition is largely an artefact of incomplete deposited annotation. Module-level surveys require re-annotation before comparative claims.
