Language practice 3 decks · two languages · free, no account

Decks I built to study with

Three flashcard apps written for my own language study and kept public. Each one runs entirely in the browser: your progress is stored on your device, nothing is sent anywhere, and none of them needs an account.

All three teach a language to Indonesian speakers, so their interface and every translation on the cards are in Indonesian.

The three decks

  • 469 cards · 18 situations

    会話 · Kaiwa

    Everyday Japanese: convenience stores, trains, clinics, city offices, banks, the post office, haircuts and the workplace. Includes speech playback and a listening mode that hides the text.

    Search · Audio · Speaker/listener filter
  • 56 cards · 9 categories

    敬語 · Keigo

    Japanese honorific speech for working life, separated into 尊敬語 (sonkeigo), 謙譲語 (kenjōgo) and 丁寧語 (teineigo), plus cards for the mistakes people actually make.

    Verb pairs · Email · Phone · Apologies
  • 5 modules · 188 vocabulary entries

    الفصحى · Arabic Fusha

    Modern Standard Arabic for readers who already know the script and its vowel marks but not yet the words. Vocabulary, grammar, root families, and reading text stripped of its harakat.

    Leitner boxes · Root patterns · Worksheets

How they work

  • Progress stays on your device. Cards you mark as learned are remembered in the browser's own storage. There is no sign-in, no server and nothing to delete later.
  • The training wheels come off. Japanese furigana sits above the kanji as real <ruby> markup and can be switched off; the Arabic deck can hide harakat the same way, turning any vocabulary card into a reading exercise.
  • Built to be read aloud. Kaiwa speaks each line with the browser's Japanese voice, at normal or slow speed.
  • Spaced repetition where it helps. The Arabic deck uses five Leitner boxes and can show only the cards that are due.
  • Fast and offline-friendly. Themes are drawn purely in CSS and the data loads from plain scripts, so the decks work from a local file and keep working once cached.

One caveat on the Arabic deck

Its material was drafted with a language model and checked automatically only on what a machine can check. The Arabic itself has not yet been reviewed by a native speaker or a teacher, so treat it as study material to verify against a dictionary rather than as a reference. Items I am unsure about are listed openly in the repository.
