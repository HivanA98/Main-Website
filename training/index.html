Language practice 2 decks · 525 cards · free and offline-capable

Japanese practice decks

Two flashcard apps I built for my own Japanese study and kept public. Every card carries furigana over the kanji, romaji, and an Indonesian translation; progress is stored in the browser, so nothing needs an account.

Both decks teach Japanese to Indonesian speakers, so their interface and all card translations are in Indonesian.

The two decks

  • 469 cards · 18 situations

    会話 · Kaiwa

    Everyday conversation: convenience stores, trains, clinics, city offices, banks, the post office, haircuts and the workplace. Includes speech playback and a listening mode that hides the text.

    Search · Audio · Speaker/listener filter
  • 56 cards · 9 categories

    敬語 · Keigo

    Honorific speech for working life, separated into 尊敬語 (sonkeigo), 謙譲語 (kenjōgo) and 丁寧語 (teineigo), plus cards for the mistakes people actually make.

    Verb pairs · Email · Phone · Apologies

How they work

  • Furigana you can switch off. Readings sit above the kanji as real <ruby> markup; hide them to test yourself.
  • Progress stays on your device. Cards you mark as learned are remembered in the browser, with no sign-in and no server.
  • Built to be read aloud. Kaiwa speaks each line with the browser's Japanese voice, at normal or slow speed.
  • Three themes — washi, indigo and matcha — drawn purely in CSS, so the decks load fast and work offline once cached.
